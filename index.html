About

A practitioner's view of emergency management

In a small, unincorporated community, the volunteer fire department is often the whole local emergency system. As Fire Chief of the SCVFD, I was responsible for the full cycle: preventing incidents through public education, keeping volunteers trained and equipped, leading the response when calls came in, and supporting the community after an event.

That work meant coordinating with provincial partners such as the Office of the Fire Marshal, the Ministry of Natural Resources and neighbouring departments through mutual aid. It also meant communicating clearly with residents during floods and fire bans, and keeping an all-volunteer organization ready on a limited budget.

This site supports my application to Emergency Management Ontario for the role of Team Lead (Fire Services Advisor 2), Job ID 248416. It shows how that front-line experience applies to provincial emergency management.

Experience by EM component

Experience mapped to Ontario's emergency management framework

Department activities grouped by the five components of emergency management: prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response and recovery.

01 · Prevention & Mitigation

Public fire and life-safety education

  • Ran annual Fire Prevention Week campaigns with the NFPA, including kitchen-fire prevention and monthly smoke alarm testing.
  • Promoted CO alarm compliance under Ontario's Hawkins-Gignac Act, which made alarms mandatory in homes with fuel-burning appliances.
  • Delivered the OFM Public Fire Safety Council's "12 Days of Holiday Fire Safety" campaign locally.
  • Ran the community's fire permit program and gave out free smoke alarms at fire hall open houses.

02 · Preparedness

Capability, training and resources

  • Recruited new volunteers and put them through OFM / Ontario Fire College Module A training, covering incident management, SCBA, PPE, suppression, ventilation and firefighter survival.
  • Restored water-supply capacity after the department's tanker was lost: found, imported and put a replacement tanker into service within about four months.
  • Took part in regional mutual aid planning through the Algoma Mutual Aid Association, including the Mutual Aid Conference.
  • Took part in MNRF's annual Goulais River pre-flood working group. The SCVFD is the designated lead agency for on-the-ground flood response in Searchmont.
  • Helped replace the 25-year-old radio repeater that links Sault Ste. Marie dispatch to fire units up to 100 km away, through a joint grant with three neighbouring departments.
  • Maintained apparatus, the volunteer Green Light program and radio communications.

03 · Response

Incident command and public warning

  • Led the response to fire and first-response calls across a rural area with long travel distances and limited water supply.
  • Passed MNR flood bulletins to residents during Goulais River spring flooding, including road closures, roads under water and emergency contacts.
  • Announced and enforced provincial Restricted Fire Zone orders during high wildfire-hazard periods, and announced when they were lifted.
  • Opened the community hall as a reception and warming centre for 27 passengers and 3 crew evacuated from a train stopped by track washouts in October 2014.
  • Turned the April 2012 CN derailment into rail-response lesson plans and mock training scenarios. CN Rail later used the response as example material in its Railroad Emergency Response Management course.
  • Yearly average of 45 calls ranging across medical, structure fire, motor vehicle collisions, wildland fires, search and rescue support, snowmobile trail responses, hazardous material incidents, railway incident responses

04 · Recovery & Community Resilience

Sustaining the department and the community

  • Organized the annual Fireman's Ball from 2010 on to raise funds and thank volunteers.
  • Held fire hall open houses and took part in Searchmont Community Day and local festivals to build trust and recruit volunteers.
  • Supported residents after fires and floods: connected families with the Canadian Red Cross and social services, checked in on affected households and helped with cleanup.
  • Worked with the Local Services Board and Recreation Club to open the Community Centre to displaced residents, with showers, a kitchen and a clean, warm, safe place to start recovering.
  • Held weekly debriefs on every call focused on continuous improvement: recognizing what went well and agreeing on areas to improve. Outside of SOG and legislative changes, these debriefs were the main driver of change in the department.

Cultural understanding

Reconciliation in practice

My family heritage is Indigenous. My great-grandmother was Anishinaabe from Batchewana. I don't hold status myself, but that heritage shapes how I approach my work and my community.

I have close connections with several Indigenous-led community groups. As President of the Superior Community Skatepark Association, I work with our volunteers to keep our local skatepark safe, clean and open to everyone.

I completed Algoma University's profound Grow with Gabegendaadowin training experience. It covered Anishinaabe culture, treaty relationships, the Indian Act and related policy, intergenerational trauma and resilience, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's Calls to Action, and personal reconciliation. The time spent was an immersive, open experience of learning to walk in someone else's shoes.

Working with First Nations through mutual aid

  • Through the Algoma Mutual Aid Association, the department worked directly with Mississauga First Nation, which played a key role in supplying apparatus for regional training events.
  • Several neighbouring departments provide fire services to First Nations under contract. Through our mutual aid agreements, the SCVFD stands ready to support those communities when needed.

Washouts · Rescue · Rail evacuation · October 14, 2014

A night of washouts, a ladder rescue and a train evacuation on Highway 532

Situation
Heavy rain in the Wabos area caused several washouts. On the way to a fire call, crews found a hole in Highway 532 the size of a transport truck, 20 feet deep with a straight drop-off. More washouts left a man stranded between two gaps in the road. Water had also undermined the track, so a southbound Algoma Central Railway train was stopped at Wabos.
My role
Fire Chief and Incident Commander, SCVFD.
Actions
Secured the washout so no vehicle could drive into it, and confirmed with the callers that there was no fire. Bridged the gap with a ladder so the stranded man could crawl across to safety, with rope rigging from Sault Search and Rescue as a safety line. Worked with ACR, CN and Search and Rescue to bring 27 passengers and 3 crew to Searchmont by hi-rail vehicle. Opened the Searchmont community centre for shelter, food and drink until buses took everyone on to Sault Ste. Marie.
Outcome
The stranded man and all 30 people from the train were brought to safety. The response involved several agencies working together through the night. Lesson learned: a clearer, more detailed understanding of what Sault Search and Rescue's rope-rescue-trained members can provide. Their rigging made the ladder crossing much safer, and that capability now informs how we plan for and call on them.

Media coverage: SooToday, "Dramatic night of rescues in Searchmont" (October 15, 2014)

Structure fire · Incident command · Mutual aid · February 2019

Lakeside house fire reached by a narrow private road in winter

Situation
Paged at about 3:30 a.m. for a shed fire. Wind and the shed's closeness to the house spread the fire quickly. The only access was a narrow, poorly maintained winter private road on the north end of the lake, in sub-zero temperatures.
My role
Fire Chief and Incident Commander.
Actions
Called in mutual aid from the Aweres Fire Department, 30 minutes away, for more firefighters and equipment. The road could take only one tanker load, so crews cut a hole in the lake ice and drafted with a portable pump. Water was kept flowing constantly so hoses and nozzles wouldn't freeze. An aggressive knockdown and defensive exposure protection kept the fire from reaching other homes. The Red Cross was dispatched to support the displaced homeowner and family.
Outcome
No injuries. The house and shed were a total loss, but the fire did not spread to nearby homes. The house next door had only minor heat damage to its siding.

Media coverage: SooToday, "Fire destroys Searchmont area home" (SCVFD news release, February 13, 2019)

Rail derailment · Hazardous materials · April 1, 2012

CN freight train derailment: 11 of 34 cars off the track

Situation
On Sunday morning, 11 cars of a 34-car train derailed near Searchmont on the CN line between Sault Ste. Marie and Hearst. Cars, wheel sets and a dozen or so steel coils went down the embankment into the woods, creating a hazardous waste scene. The derailment affected a main tributary of the Goulais River and brought contamination into contact with the local water supply. That was a serious concern in an area where residents rely on private wells.
My role
Led the department's after-action review and the training changes that came out of it.
Actions
The after-action review found a notification gap: CN had not informed the department as its own procedures required. Railway response content was added to individual lesson plans where it applied, and the derailment became the base data for mock scenarios in later training sessions.
Outcome
The line reopened the next morning, sooner than CN expected. Rail incidents became a standing part of the department's training plan. CN Rail later used the SCVFD's response as example material in its Railroad Emergency Response Management course.

Media coverage: SooToday, "Searchmont derailment update" (April 3, 2012), with photos taken by Travis McCormack

Flood · October 10–11, 2018

Goulais River localized flooding

Situation
Heavy fall rain sent the Goulais River and its tributaries over roads across the response area. Roads were closed, and apparatus had to drive through water-covered sections.
My role
Fire Chief and Incident Commander. Under MNRF's Goulais River flood plan, the SCVFD is the lead agency for on-the-ground flood response in Searchmont.
Actions
Staffed flooded-road closures overnight alongside the OPP, and kept apparatus moving through flooded sections to maintain coverage. Coordinated evacuations with the OPP and Sault Search and Rescue. Used the local Vulnerable Persons Registry (VPR) to find residents who might need help, and encouraged residents we met to add themselves to it.
Outcome
Flooding in the Goulais River watershed happens almost every year, with fall rains and spring runoff. The real question each year is how severe it will be. Response depends on preparation done well before the water rises.
Year-round flood planning
Took part in the annual pre-flood working group led by MNRF, bringing together the OPP, MTO, Algoma Power, the Red Cross, Sault Search and Rescue, the environment ministry and neighbouring fire departments. MNRF shared flood outlook briefs, and the department reported back on conditions on the ground. In one year, we reported a power pole whose footing had washed out. The group connected us directly with Algoma Power to schedule a replacement pole. That line is the community's only power feed, and losing it would have cut power to hundreds of households.
